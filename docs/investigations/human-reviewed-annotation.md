### Who performs each pass

| Stage | Responsible party | What that person may inspect | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First pass | **Reviewer A**, a human annotator | Source frames and the frozen annotation policy; optional assistance, but no candidate-detector predictions | A complete `reviewer-a.json` |
| Second pass | **Reviewer B**, a different human | The same source frames and policy, but not Reviewer A's file, its comparison report, or candidate-detector predictions | A separately initialized and complete `reviewer-b.json` |
| Adjudication | **Adjudicator C**, a third qualified human who performed neither pass | Source frames, the policy, both completed reviewer files, and their comparison report; no candidate-detector predictions | The merged fixture and an entry for every resolved disagreement or genuine ambiguity |

An AI model, browser suggestion tool, or annotation agent may assist a human, but it
cannot be Reviewer A, Reviewer B, or Adjudicator C and cannot add to
`review.independent_passes`. If no third qualified human is available to adjudicate,
the fixture remains incomplete; neither independent reviewer may approve their own
disputed labels.

This snapshot records completion state but no reviewer identity, so it proves that
one manual pass was marked complete, not **who** completed it. Operationally, treat
the person who produced this saved file as Reviewer A. Record the actual people
assigned to all three roles in access-controlled provenance outside the anonymized
fixture, and never infer identity from a browser session, cookie, or filename.

### Why NanoDet is not the second pass

The two passes are two independent **human ground-truth passes**. NanoDet, or any
other detector under investigation, produces a prediction set after the human truth
has been frozen; it does not increment `review.independent_passes`. Comparing one
human's labels with NanoDet would measure human/model disagreement, but it could not
tell which side was correct. Letting the evaluated model supply half of its own
ground truth would also make its reported precision and recall circular.

If the immediate goal is only exploratory triage, the repository may compare this
single human pass with NanoDet and report the disagreements for another human to
inspect. That output must be labeled a **provisional human/model disagreement
audit**, not a second independent pass, adjudicated ground truth, or detector
accuracy result.

An AI assistant may generate boxes for Reviewer A or Reviewer B to accept, reject,
or redraw, but the responsible human must inspect every frame. That remains one
human pass, and a different human must still complete the other pass without seeing
the first pass or the candidate detector's predictions.

Likewise, an AI assistant—including this repository's coding assistant—may run the
comparison, list disagreements, and prepare evidence for review, but it is not the
adjudicator. Adjudicator C is the human accountable for deciding the final class,
subset, and box from the source pixels and frozen policy. Only after that decision,
validation, and fixture freeze may NanoDet be scored against the fixture:

```text
Human Reviewer A ─┐
                  ├─> comparison ─> Human Adjudicator C ─> frozen ground truth
Human Reviewer B ─┘                                            │
                                                               ├─> NanoDet score
NanoDet predictions (kept hidden during review) ────────────────┘
```
